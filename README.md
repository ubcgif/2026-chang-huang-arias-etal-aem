# Airborne Electromagnetic Characterization of Shallow Permafrost in the Canadian Arctic for Infrastructure Challenges

**Authors:** Pablo Chang Huang Arias<sup>1</sup>, Marco A. Couto Jr.<sup>1</sup>, Lindsey J. Heagy<sup>1</sup>, Duane G. Froese<sup>2</sup>, Alejandro Alvarez<sup>2</sup>, Steve V. Kokelj<sup>3</sup>

## Abstract

Permafrost degradation along the Inuvik–Tuktoyaktuk Highway (ITH), a 138 km all-season road crossing ice-rich continuous permafrost in the Northwest Territories, Canada, increasingly threatens infrastructure and northern communities. In August 2024, a 1,142 line-km airborne frequency-domain electromagnetic (AEM) survey was flown along the ITH, the longest of its kind for continuous-permafrost applications. In this resistive setting, the low-frequency data were often unusable because they fell below the noise floor, so the usable AEM data are most sensitive to the uppermost few metres. We therefore developed a shallow-focused SimPEG inversion workflow that combines sounding-specific reference models from a high-frequency inversion with depth-varying regularization. This approach promotes structure in the shallow layers, keeps the deeper structure more uniform as expected in this setting, and makes the most of the usable frequencies. At Hans Creek Bridge, the resistivity models show that the western side of the bridge is more conductive, and therefore warmer, than the eastern side, consistent with borehole temperatures and with the recent intervention on that side. At Zed Creek, where road construction rerouted surface flow, low resistivities extend to ~10 m depth, with the lowest values along the new flow path and in ponded areas where the road is degrading, in agreement with field and remote sensing observations. These results demonstrate a methodology for using AEM as a rapid, large-scale and cost-effective tool to characterize shallow permafrost and support infrastructure and hazard planning in remote Arctic regions.
