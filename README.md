# Airborne Electromagnetic Characterization of Shallow Permafrost in the Canadian Arctic for Infrastructure Challenges

Permafrost degradation along the Inuvik–Tuktoyaktuk Highway (ITH), a 138 km all-season road crossing ice-rich continuous permafrost in the Northwest Territories, Canada, increasingly threatens infrastructure and northern communities. In August 2024, a 1,142 line-km airborne frequency-domain electromagnetic (AEM) survey was flown along the ITH, the longest of its kind for continuous-permafrost applications. Because low-frequency data are often unusable in this resistive setting and the AEM response is highly sensitive to the uppermost few metres, we developed a shallow-focused SimPEG inversion workflow. It combines sounding-specific reference models from a high-frequency active-layer inversion with depth-varying regularization. At Hans Creek Bridge, the resulting resistivity models show a more conductive, warmer western side, consistent with borehole temperatures and supporting the thermosyphon intervention there. At Zed Creek, where road construction rerouted surface flow, low resistivities extend to ~10 m depth, and they are lowest along the new flow path and ponded areas, in agreement with field and remote sensing observations. These results show that AEM is a rapid, large-scale and cost-effective tool for characterizing shallow permafrost and supporting infrastructure and hazard planning in remote Arctic regions
